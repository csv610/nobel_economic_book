\chapter{1979 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Theodore Schultz (USA), Arthur Lewis (UK)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Pioneering studies economic development

\section{What They Did}
Theodore Schultz emphasized importance human capital agriculture development poor countries investment education health productivity labor Arthur Lewis created dual-sector model of economic development explaining structural transformation low-productivity agriculture higher-productivity industry migration surplus labor urbanization Lewis's model described transition developing economy agricultural surplus wage equalization process industrialization driven migration

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
 Their work shifted focus from merely physical capital accumulation to human capabilities and structural change laying foundation development economics informed agricultural investment rural development policies industrialization strategies Schultz argued investments education health same productive investment machinery Lewis analysis described how growing industrial sectors absorb agricultural surplus creating self-reinforcing development cycle Their work justified targeted development interventions recognizing agriculture critical sector early development

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Human capital theory became central education policy labor economics Lewis's model informs structural transformation development indicators incorporate human development capability approach gender dimensions added to development analysis Dual-process models continue informing policy regarding rural-urban migration industrialization sequencing Human capital returns estimation guides education spending decisions Understanding complementary roles agriculture industry remains relevant today especially Sub-Saharan Africa South Asia undergoing similar transitions

\chapter{1991 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Ronald Coase (UK/USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Discovery and clarification significance of transaction costs property rights

\section{What They Did}
Ronald Coase made two fundamental contributions first analyzing why firms exist and determining boundaries through transaction cost analysis arguing firms emerge when market transactions costs exceed internal coordination costs His famous theorem demonstrated that with zero transaction costs resource allocation independent of initial property assignments whereas with positive transaction costs legal rules determine efficient outcomes This insight transformed understanding economic institutions second he analyzed regulation examining how government interventions often produce unintended consequences due to information asymmetries and incentive problems His empirical approach to institutional economics showed that institutions matter profoundly for economic performance opening new research directions about law economics regulation business organization

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Coase revolutionized understanding of economic institutions by introducing transaction cost analysis as central analytical framework His work explained why certain economic activities occur within firms rather than markets establishing boundary theory guiding corporate restructuring privatization mergers acquisitions decisions Coase theorem influenced law and economics field providing systematic analysis legal rules efficiency property rights arrangements contract enforcement dispute resolution Regulatory capture phenomenon explained through Coasian lens showing industries influence regulation for private benefit Public choice economists incorporate transaction costs into political analysis Institutional development economists examine historical paths dependency on initial property rights assignments Contemporary contract law intellectual property regulation environmental policy design all draw upon Coase insights about efficient risk allocation minimizing waste through appropriate liability regimes

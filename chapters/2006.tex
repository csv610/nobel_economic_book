\chapter{2006 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Edmund Phelps (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Intertemporal trade-offs in macroeconomic policy

\section{What They Did}
Edmund Phelps developed the natural rate of unemployment hypothesis, distinguishing cyclical unemployment from structurally persistent unemployment. The Phillips curve trade-off exists only temporarily as expectations adjust, returning unemployment to its natural rate. Adaptive expectations gradually incorporate past inflation experiences, while rational expectations fully anticipate policy changes, immediately rendering any temporary trade-off irrelevant. Phelps also developed the theory of intertemporal macroeconomic policy, examining how current decisions affect future welfare. His work on consumption-saving decisions across lifetimes, investment choices affecting future productive capacity, and the long-run implications of monetary-fiscal policy established the foundation for understanding sustainable growth patterns rather than temporary stimulus effects.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Phelps' natural rate hypothesis fundamentally transformed macroeconomic understanding of the inflation-unemployment relationship. It demonstrated that monetary policy cannot permanently reduce unemployment below the natural rate; stimulating demand merely accelerates inflation. The distinction between cyclical and structural unemployment became crucial for policy formulation. Central banks adopted the natural rate concept as a cornerstone of macroeconomic analysis, recognizing that structural reforms—education, training, labor market flexibility—are necessary to address persistent unemployment. The expectations-augmented Phillips curve became standard in economics textbooks, clarifying the limits of accommodative monetary policy and emphasizing the importance of anchoring inflation expectations.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
The natural rate hypothesis continues to inform central bank forward guidance and communication strategies. Modern monetary policy frameworks anchor expectations about future policy paths, preventing destabilizing speculation. Unemployment gap estimation around the natural rate guides monetary easing and tightening decisions. Fiscal policy analysis examines sustainability implications of borrowing and spending. The concept has been extended to analyze labor market dynamics, including gig economy regulation, occupational licensing reforms, and the effects of minimum wage policies. Contemporary research explores how structural factors—demographic changes, technological disruption, globalization—shift the natural rate over time, making Phelps' framework essential for understanding long-run economic performance.

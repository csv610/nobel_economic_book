\chapter{2015 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Angus Deaton (UK/USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Analysis of consumption, poverty, and welfare

\section{What They Did}
Angus Deaton made groundbreaking contributions to the analysis of consumption, poverty, and welfare. He developed the almost ideal demand system, a flexible functional form for estimating consumer demand that became standard in empirical industrial organization and policy analysis. Deaton's work on household survey methodology improved how economists measure poverty and living standards in developing countries. He analyzed the relationship between consumption, income, and wealth, showing how permanent income fluctuations affect current consumption. His research on health economics examined how health shocks affect consumption patterns and poverty dynamics. Deaton also studied the political economy of development, analyzing how government policies affect poverty reduction and welfare.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Deaton transformed consumption theory and poverty measurement. The almost ideal demand system provided researchers with practical tools for analyzing consumer behavior and evaluating policy impacts. His work on household surveys established standards for collecting and analyzing microdata in developing countries. Deaton's analysis of consumption smoothing showed how households manage risk through saving, borrowing, and social networks. His research on the macroeconomics of health connected individual health decisions to aggregate economic outcomes. Deaton's later work on inequality examined how wealth and income distributions affect political institutions and economic performance. His interdisciplinary approach bridged economics, demography, epidemiology, and political science, enriching all these fields.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Deaton's methodological contributions remain central to empirical economics. The almost ideal demand system is widely used in consumer research, policy evaluation, and industrial organization. His poverty measurement frameworks inform international development policy and World Bank analyses. Research on consumption smoothing continues to explore how financial development, social insurance, and credit constraints affect household welfare. Deaton's work on health economics influences health policy debates about insurance coverage, preventive care, and health inequality. His recent research on inequality and democracy has sparked debates about the relationship between economic concentration and political power. As global inequality and health disparities persist, Deaton's analytical tools remain essential for understanding and addressing these challenges.

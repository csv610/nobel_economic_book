\textbf{Laureates: }Maurice Allais (France)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Fundamental pioneering contributions theory markets

\section{What They Did}
Maurice Allais developed general equilibrium theory emphasizing production processes contributed to monetary dynamics and risk theory Known for Allais paradox demonstrating violations expected utility theory preference reversals depending framing options His work showed people prefer certain outcomes probabilistic ones even when expected values identical challenging foundational expected utility assumption Allais contributed to monetary theory analyzing quantity of money effects on economic activity Developed comprehensive system monetary economics connecting money prices investment employment His production theory emphasized technical coefficients determining factor proportions in production Allais provided rigorous treatment equilibrium under uncertainty anticipating developments in behavioral economics

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Allais paradox prefigured Kahneman prospect theory challenging expected utility dominance in decision making Showing systematic violations indicated humans do not maximize expected utility in ways assumed by classical theory His work prompted extensive research into alternative decision theories rank-dependent utility cumulative prospect theory behavioral economics emerging decades later Allais contributions to monetary theory provided early systematic analysis monetary aggregates price levels inflation-output relationships His general equilibrium approach emphasizing production processes influenced later work integrating production into comprehensive equilibrium models Allais anticipated many developments in behavioral macroeconomics his work received late recognition appropriate his pioneering status

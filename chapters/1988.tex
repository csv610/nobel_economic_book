\chapter{1988 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Maurice Allais (France)

\section*{Reason for Award}
For his pioneering contributions to the theory of markets and efficient
utilization of resources

\section{What They Did}
Maurice Allais developed general equilibrium theory emphasizing production
processes. He contributed to monetary dynamics and risk theory. He is known for
the Allais paradox demonstrating violations of expected utility theory.

His work showed people prefer certain outcomes to probabilistic ones even when
expected values are identical. This challenged the foundational expected utility
assumption in decision theory. Allais contributed to monetary theory by
analyzing quantity of money effects on economic activity.

He developed a comprehensive system of monetary economics connecting money,
prices, investment, and employment. His production theory emphasized technical
coefficients determining factor proportions in production.

Allais provided rigorous treatment of equilibrium under uncertainty, anticipating
developments in behavioral economics. He earned his doctorate at the École
Polytechnique and the Sorbonne. His 1943 thesis developed a general equilibrium
framework incorporating production and uncertainty.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Allais paradox prefigured Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory. It challenged
expected utility dominance in decision making and showed systematic violations of
rational choice assumptions. His work prompted extensive research into alternative
decision theories.

Rank-dependent utility and cumulative prospect theory emerged as alternatives to
expected utility. Behavioral economics emerged decades later, building on Allais's
insights about systematic deviations from expected utility maximization.

Allais contributions to monetary theory provided early systematic analysis of
monetary aggregates and price levels. His analysis of inflation-output
relationships influenced monetary economics. His general equilibrium approach
emphasizing production processes influenced later work integrating production
into comprehensive equilibrium models.

The Allais paradox became one of the most cited empirical findings in economics
history. It challenged the normative status of expected utility theory.
Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory incorporated the Allais paradox as a key
empirical motivation.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Behavioral economics incorporates Allais insights showing systematic deviations
from expected utility. Rational expectations models incorporate psychological
realism based on his findings.

The Allais paradox is frequently cited illustrating limitations of expected
utility theory. Modern decision theory accommodates probability weighting and
loss aversion he identified. Allais's monetary analysis influenced monetary
economics and price stickiness research.

Macro models incorporating behavioral elements reflect his early contributions
to the field. His legacy is visible in the increasing acceptance of realistic
human behavior in economic models.

Monetary policy analysis now considers expectations formation and psychological
factors. His early warnings about money-price relations resonate during inflation
episodes. Allais recognized the importance of integrating decision-making errors
into macroeconomic fluctuations. His broader legacy includes his advocacy for
scientific rigor and intellectual independence. He challenged established
doctrines and proposed alternative frameworks that expanded economic analysis.

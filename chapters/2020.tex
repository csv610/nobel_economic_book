\chapter{2020 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Paul Milgrom (USA), Robert Wilson (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
For improvements to auction theory and inventions of new auction formats

\section{What They Did}
Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson advanced auction theory and designed new auction
formats that have been implemented worldwide, generating billions in government
revenue.

Wilson developed the common value auction model, showing how bidders' estimates
of an item's true value are correlated. In common value auctions, the item
has the same true value for all bidders, but each bidder receives a private
signal about that value. Wilson identified the winner's curse, where the
winner tends to overpay because their estimate is optimistically biased.
Rational bidders must shade their bids to account for this tendency.

Milgrom extended the analysis to include private values and strategic
interactions. He developed the exposure problem for bidders in multi-item
auctions, where winning some items but not complementary ones can lead to
losses. This insight informed the design of spectrum auctions.

Together, they designed the simultaneous multiple round auction (SMRA), which
allows bidders to express preferences across multiple items while revealing
information through bidding patterns. The SMRA combines the information
revealing properties of English auctions with the efficiency of simultaneous
bidding.

Milgrom later developed the combinatorial clock auction, which allows bidders
to express preferences for packages of items. This addressed the exposure
problem more effectively and became the basis for modern spectrum auctions.
The ascending proxy auction, developed by Milgrom and Weber, improved
further on these designs.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Milgrom and Wilson's work transformed auction theory from an academic curiosity
to a practical tool for market design. Their theoretical insights explained
why different auction formats yield different outcomes and revenues.

The winner's curse concept became standard in economics education and practice.
Investors learn to avoid the winner's curse in oil leasing, artwork purchases,
and other common value settings.

Their auction designs have raised hundreds of billions of dollars for
governments while promoting competition and efficiency. The FCC spectrum
auctions, which began in 1994, generated over \$100 billion in revenue.
Similar auctions have been adopted worldwide for telecommunications spectrum.

The work influenced antitrust policy, merger analysis, and regulatory design.
Market design has emerged as a distinct subfield of economics, with
applications ranging from organ allocation to school choice programs.

Milgrom and Wilson showed how auction theory connects to other areas of
economics, including game theory, industrial organization, and mechanism design.
Their practical contributions demonstrated how economic theory can solve
real-world problems.

Wilson's analysis of the winner's curse has become a standard concept in
economics education. The insight that the winner in a common value auction
tends to overpay has implications for oil leasing, artwork purchases, and
any situation where bidders have imperfect information about value.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Auction theory based on Milgrom and Wilson's work continues to evolve and expand.
Spectrum auctions remain the most famous application, with formats refined over
decades. The FCC has adopted increasingly sophisticated designs to address
emerging challenges.

Online advertising auctions, where Google and Facebook earn most revenue, use
variations of their theoretical insights. Generalized second-price auctions
and Vickrey-Clarke-Groves mechanisms are standard in digital advertising.

Carbon emission trading systems apply auction design principles to environmental
policy. The European Union Emissions Trading System uses auctions to allocate
carbon allowances, generating significant revenue while controlling emissions.

Electricity markets use auction formats to balance supply and demand in real
time. Day-ahead and real-time markets use combinatorial auctions to ensure
reliability while minimizing costs.

Cryptocurrency auctions, Initial Coin Offerings, and NFT sales represent new
applications of auction theory. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the importance
of auction design for vaccine distribution and resource allocation.

As new market designs emerge, Milgrom and Wilson's theoretical foundations
remain essential. Research continues to address challenges in multi-sided
markets, dynamic pricing, and algorithmic auction design.

\chapter{2020 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Paul Milgrom (USA), Robert Wilson (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Improvements to auction theory and inventions of new auction formats

\section{What They Did}
Paul Milgrom and Robert Wilson advanced auction theory and designed new auction formats that have been implemented worldwide. Wilson developed the common value auction model, showing how bidders' estimates of an item's true value are correlated. He identified the winner's curse, where the winner tends to overpay because their estimate is optimistically biased. Milgrom extended the analysis to include private values and strategic interactions, developing the exposure problem for bidders in multi-item auctions. Together, they designed the simultaneous multiple round auction, which allows bidders to express preferences across multiple items while revealing information through bidding patterns. This format has been used for spectrum auctions, emissions trading, and electricity markets.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Milgrom and Wilson's work transformed auction theory from an academic curiosity to a practical tool for market design. Their theoretical insights explained why different auction formats yield different outcomes and revenues. The winner's curse concept became standard in economics education and practice. Their auction designs have raised hundreds of billions of dollars for governments while promoting competition and efficiency. The work influenced antitrust policy, merger analysis, and regulatory design. Milgrom and Wilson showed how auction theory connects to other areas of economics, including game theory, industrial organization, and mechanism design. Their practical contributions demonstrated how economic theory can solve real-world problems.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Auction theory based on Milgrom and Wilson's work continues to evolve and expand. Spectrum auctions remain the most famous application, with formats refined over decades. Online advertising auctions, where Google and Facebook earn most revenue, use variations of their theoretical insights. Carbon emission trading systems apply auction design principles to environmental policy. Electricity markets use auction formats to balance supply and demand in real time. Cryptocurrency auctions, Initial Coin Offerings, and NFT sales represent new applications of auction theory. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the importance of auction design for vaccine distribution and resource allocation. As new market designs emerge, Milgrom and Wilson's theoretical foundations remain essential.

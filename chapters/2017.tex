\chapter{2017 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Richard Thaler (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
For his contributions to behavioral economics

\section{What They Did}
Richard Thaler advanced behavioral economics by incorporating psychological
insights into economic analysis. He identified systematic deviations from
rational behavior, including mental accounting, where people treat money
differently depending on its source or intended use.

Thaler documented the endowment effect, showing that people value items more
highly simply because they own them. He analyzed present bias, demonstrating
how people discount future rewards more steeply than exponential discounting
predicts.

Thaler's concept of nudging showed how choice architecture can help people make
better decisions without restricting freedom. He applied these insights to
savings behavior, retirement planning, and consumer protection.

His book "Misbehaving" traced the development of behavioral economics as a field.
Thaler showed that traditional economic assumptions about rationality are too
restrictive and that realistic models of human behavior improve prediction and
policy design.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Thaler transformed economics by showing that traditional assumptions about
rationality are too restrictive. His work demonstrated that people have limited
self-control, limited forethought, and limited sympathy for others.

These insights explained puzzles that standard economics could not, such as low
retirement savings, procrastination, and loss aversion. Thaler's nudging concept
influenced policy design worldwide, leading to automatic enrollment in retirement
plans, organ donor registration, and healthier food choices in cafeterias.

His work bridged economics and psychology, creating a more realistic foundation
for understanding human behavior. The recognition of bounded rationality led to
more humane and effective policy interventions.

Thaler's contributions earned him recognition as a pioneer who made economics
more relevant to real-world problems. He demonstrated that economics can be both
scientifically rigorous and practically useful.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Behavioral economics has become mainstream, with Thaler's insights influencing
policy, business, and academic research. Automatic enrollment programs have
dramatically increased retirement savings rates.

Nudge units in governments worldwide apply behavioral insights to improve policy
outcomes. Financial products increasingly incorporate behavioral considerations,
such as default options and simplified disclosures.

The field has expanded to address climate change, public health, and education
policy. Thaler's recent work on eudaimonic economics explores how well-being and
flourishing can guide policy beyond traditional welfare measures.

As behavioral economics matures, its insights continue to reshape how economists
understand decision-making, design institutions, and evaluate policy
interventions.

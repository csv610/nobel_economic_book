\chapter{1982 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }George Stigler (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Structure functioning industries

\section{What They Did}
George Stigler founded regulatory capture theory suggesting regulated industries capture regulators serving narrow interests rather than public interest Developed industrial organization economics through empirical study of market structures firm behaviors championed Chicago school emphasizing free markets minimal government intervention Conducted seminal analysis advertising information provision consumer welfare Contributed to understanding entry barriers concentration effects on efficiency His rigorous application of economic theory to legal-institutional environments established law and economics field

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Stigler fundamentally changed how economists view regulation showing often creates rent-seeking opportunities benefiting insiders rather than improving social welfare His empirical approach to industrial organization provided evidence-based analysis of market performance shifting focus from abstract theorization to concrete measurement Concentration performance debate examined links between market power and efficiency showing mixed results challenging simplistic pro-concentration positions His work informed antitrust policy emphasizing consumer welfare effects concentrating on allocative efficiency productive efficiency innovation incentives

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Regulatory capture theory remains central political economy and regulation studies Applied extensively telecommunications utilities environment financial regulation Law and economics field has grown substantially influenced by Stigler s approach Antitrust enforcement increasingly focuses on empirical efficiency analysis Stiglers emphasis on clear thinking and empirical verification influences contemporary industrial organization research Regulatory reform movements cite his insights questioning necessity of various regulations Industry analysis continues build on Stigler framework examining entry barriers product differentiation advertising strategies in competitive markets

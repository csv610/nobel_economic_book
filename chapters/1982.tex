\chapter{1982 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }George Stigler (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
For his seminal studies of industrial structures, functioning of markets and causes and effects of public regulation

\section{What They Did}
George Stigler founded regulatory capture theory, suggesting that regulated industries often capture their regulators, serving narrow interests rather than the public interest. He developed industrial organization economics through empirical study of market structures and firm behaviors, championing the Chicago school approach that emphasized free markets and minimal government intervention. Stigler conducted seminal analysis of advertising and information provision, showing how markets generate information signals. He contributed to understanding entry barriers and concentration effects on efficiency. His rigorous application of economic theory to legal and institutional environments established the field of law and economics. Stigler's work demonstrated that regulation often benefits regulated industries at the expense of consumers, challenging conventional wisdom about government intervention improving outcomes.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Stigler fundamentally changed how economists view regulation, showing it often creates rent-seeking opportunities benefiting insiders rather than improving social welfare. His empirical approach to industrial organization provided evidence-based analysis of market performance, shifting focus from abstract theorization to concrete measurement. The concentration-performance debate examined links between market power and efficiency, showing mixed results that challenged simplistic pro- or anti-concentration positions. His work informed antitrust policy by emphasizing consumer welfare effects, concentrating on allocative efficiency, productive efficiency, and innovation incentives. Stigler established that economic analysis should apply to all institutional arrangements, including government regulation, not just markets. This democratization of economic analysis expanded the scope of the discipline dramatically.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Regulatory capture theory remains central to political economy and regulation studies, applied extensively in telecommunications, utilities, environment, and financial regulation. The law and economics field has grown substantially, influenced by Stigler's approach to analyzing legal rules through economic lens. Antitrust enforcement increasingly focuses on empirical efficiency analysis rather than structural presumptions. Stigler's emphasis on clear thinking and empirical verification influences contemporary industrial organization research. Regulatory reform movements cite his insights questioning necessity of various regulations. Industry analysis continues to build on Stigler's framework, examining entry barriers, product differentiation, and advertising strategies in competitive markets. His work continues to inform debates about the proper scope of government intervention in modern economies.

\chapter{2024 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Daron Acemoglu (Turkey/USA), Simon Johnson (UK/USA), James Robinson (UK/USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
For their studies of how institutions are formed and affect prosperity

\section{What They Did}
Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, and James Robinson studied how institutions
shape economic development and prosperity. Their collaborative work has
transformed our understanding of why some nations become rich while others
remain poor, emphasizing the role of political and economic institutions.

They developed the concept of extractive versus inclusive institutions.
Extractive institutions concentrate power and wealth in the hands of a
small elite, extracting resources from the broader population. Inclusive
institutions distribute power more broadly and create incentives for
investment, innovation, and economic participation by the many. This
distinction provided a powerful framework for understanding cross-country
income differences.

Their influential book "Why Nations Fail" (2012) argued that institutional
differences, rather than geography, culture, or ignorance, explain cross-
country income differences. They traced how colonial institutions shaped
modern economic outcomes, showing how extractive colonial regimes created
institutions that persisted after independence. The contrast between
settler colonies like the United States and Canada, which developed inclusive
institutions, and extractive colonies like those in Latin America and Africa
provided compelling historical evidence.

They also examined the role of critical junctures--major historical events
like the Black Death, the Age of Discovery, and the Industrial Revolution--
that created opportunities for institutional change. The interaction between
critical junctures and existing institutions determined whether societies
moved toward more inclusive or more extractive paths. These historical

Acemoglu, Johnson, and Robinson showed how institutional quality affects
investment incentives, innovation, and economic growth. Their work has
influenced policy debates about development, governance, and the role of
institutions in shaping economic outcomes. They demonstrated that inclusive
institutions create the incentives for innovation and investment that drive
long-run economic growth.

Their empirical work examined the relationship between institutions and
economic performance across countries and through history. They showed that
colonial origin was a strong predictor of current institutional quality and
economic development, providing evidence that institutions matter more than
geography or culture.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Their work transformed development economics by emphasizing the primacy of
institutions. Previous explanations focused on geography, culture, or policy
mistakes, but they showed how institutions create the incentives that
determine economic outcomes. This insight has influenced both academic
research and policy debates worldwide.

Their analysis of colonial history showed how initial institutional
choices have long-lasting effects. The contrast between colonies with
settler populations (which tended to develop inclusive institutions) and
those with extractive objectives (which did not) provides a powerful
framework for understanding contemporary development patterns.

Their work has influenced debates about democracy, governance, and economic
development. They showed how inclusive political institutions support
inclusive economic institutions, creating virtuous circles of prosperity.
Conversely, extractive institutions create vicious circles of poverty and
authoritarianism. This insight has informed policy debates about
democratization and governance reform.

The framework has been applied to analyze institutional change, the role of
elite interests in blocking reform, and the conditions under which inclusive
institutions emerge. Their work has influenced policy debates about foreign
aid, governance reform, and the conditions for sustainable development.

Their collaborative research program has produced a comprehensive theory
of economic development that integrates insights from history, political
science, and economics. The framework has been applied to understand
contemporary challenges like authoritarian resilience, democratic
backsliding, and the role of elites in blocking reform.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
The institutional framework continues to inform research on economic
development, political economy, and the determinants of prosperity. Recent
work has examined how digital technologies affect institutions, the role of
institutions in shaping responses to climate change, and the relationship
between institutions and inequality.

Their analysis has influenced debates about democratic backsliding, the
resilience of inclusive institutions, and the conditions for authoritarian
stability. The framework has been applied to understand the economic
implications of populism, nationalism, and the rise of strongman leaders
in various countries.

Research on institutional quality continues to inform policy debates about
development assistance, governance reform, and the role of international
institutions. Their work has influenced thinking about how to promote
inclusive institutions in countries with extractive legacies. The concept
of inclusive institutions has been applied to analyze contemporary
challenges like corruption, rule of law, and democratic governance.

The institutional approach has also been extended to analyze the economics
of corruption, the role of social norms in supporting institutions, and the
interactions between institutions and economic behavior. Their collaborative
research program continues to generate new insights about the foundations
of economic prosperity. Recent work has examined the role of institutions
in shaping responses to artificial intelligence and other technological
changes.

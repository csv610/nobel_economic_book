\chapter{1973 Nobel Prize in Economics}
\textbf{Laureates: }Wassily Leontief (USA)

\section*{Reason for Award}
Development of input-output method and its application to important economic problems

\section{What They Did}
Wassily Leontief created input-output analysis, representing the interdependencies between different sectors of an economy through matrices recording the flow of goods and services between producers and consumers. He organized information into coefficients tables capturing direct requirements per unit of output, enabling systematic analysis of how changes in final demand ripple through the entire economy, affecting employment, prices, productivity, and resource allocation. He constructed the first comprehensive input-output table for the United States economy in 1941, demonstrating the practical applicability of his approach. Leontief's method allowed economists to quantify the interconnectedness of economic sectors and predict the effects of changes in one sector on others. His work provided the first systematic framework for understanding the structure of entire economies rather than individual markets in isolation.

\section{Impact on Economic Thinking}
Input-output analysis provided the earliest comprehensive quantitative framework for understanding economic interdependence and systemic effects. Before Leontief, examination tended to focus on individual markets in isolation. His analysis demonstrated that shocks propagate across interconnected sectors, fundamentally changing understanding of economic structure and vulnerability. The technique became indispensable for regional economics, urban planning, environmental analysis, calculating pollution intensity by sector, technological forecasting, and international trade studies. Government agencies worldwide adopted input-output tables as standard analytical tools for economic planning, budgetary assessment, and impact evaluation. Leontief's approach established the principle that economies constitute integrated systems requiring coordinated analysis rather than collection of separate parts studied in isolation. His work bridged theory and practice, providing policymakers with concrete tools for economic analysis.

\section{Modern Perspectives Today}
Input-output analysis has been extensively refined and expanded, forming the backbone of economic databases internationally. Multi-regional models now capture global trade linkages, facilitating analysis of supply chains across countries. Environmental accounting integrates input-output data with ecological information to calculate embodied emissions and resource use. Computational General Equilibrium models extend Leontief's fixed-coefficient approach by allowing substitution among inputs in response to price changes. Big data and real-time tracking have enhanced input-output timeliness, supporting crisis response and monitoring during events like pandemic recovery assessments and geopolitical supply chain risk evaluation. Leontief's insight about economic interconnectivity resonates increasingly in a globally integrated economy where local disruptions transmit rapidly across continents. His legacy endures in every economic impact analysis and supply chain study conducted worldwide.
